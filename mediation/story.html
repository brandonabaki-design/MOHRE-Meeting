Story of Facts & Events

The full narrative — if you read nothing else before the call, read this.

1. How this started

I joined Al Wahda Private School on 4 September 2025 as Head of Curriculum, Teaching and Learning, on a 2-year limited contract with a 6-month probation period (Transaction No. MB298388212AE). My gross salary was AED 20,000 per month (basic AED 7,000).

In February 2026, still inside my probation period, I received an offer from another UAE employer (American International School in Abu Dhabi) — a role closer to my family. Under Article 9, Clause 3 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, an employee in probation who is moving to a new employer inside the UAE is required to give a 30-day written notice. I did exactly that.

2. The verbal spring-break conversation that prompted me to be precise

Before I sent my resignation email, I was verbally informed by HR that I would not be paid for spring break. That position is not compatible with Article 43 of the Labour Law, which calculates notice periods in calendar days — Fridays, Saturdays, public holidays, and school breaks all count.

Because of that verbal comment, I made a point of drafting my resignation carefully, quoting the specific articles, so the record was beyond ambiguity. I was not being combative; I was being precise, because the school had already told me they intended to treat the break as unpaid.

Note to supplement if helpful: "If the record does not reflect the verbal conversation about spring-break pay that I had with HR before I resigned, I can supplement the record with that."

3. The resignation email — dated, specific, legally grounded

On Thursday, 26 February 2026 at 1:27 PM, I sent my formal resignation to the Principal (Mr. Duraid Al Oubaidy), cc'ing HR (Ahmed Samy), Shada J., and VP Leila Chammas. The email:

  • Expressly invoked Article 9, Clause 3 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021.
  • Expressly invoked Article 43 of the Labour Law — notice in calendar days, including Spring Break.
  • Named Saturday, 28 March 2026 as "my final official day of employment".
  • Offered to complete the handover by Thursday 26 March, and to come in on Saturday 28 March if needed to finish anything.
  • Requested that the statutory 30-day notice be honoured in full so the work permit could be cancelled cleanly before my new employer onboarded me.
"My notice period will conclude on Saturday, March 28th, which will be my final official day of employment… As per Article 43 of the Labor Law, notice periods are calculated in calendar days, meaning my notice period will run continuously through the upcoming Spring Break closure."

4. Silence from the school — which matters legally

I never received a reply to my resignation email. No objection to 28 March as the last day. No counter-proposal. No statement that the school intended to treat a different date as the end. Nothing in writing — during the entire 30-day notice period.

Under UAE practice, an employer that receives a resignation specifying a clear end date, and raises no written objection during the notice period while accepting the employee's continued work and handover, has effectively accepted that date. You cannot wait until after the fact and unilaterally rewrite it.

5. I completed the handover and finished the notice

My role did not involve classroom teaching or student grading, so the operational handover of curriculum responsibilities was fully completed by 26 March as offered. I remained available throughout the notice period and confirmed in writing that I was available through end of day 28 March. The school raised no performance, handover or attendance issue.

6. The signed clearance form — the single most important document

On the morning of Tuesday, 31 March 2026 at 10:27 AM, HR (Ahmed Samy) emailed me the clearance form for review and signature. On the form — in HR's own handwriting — the school filled in:

  • Date of Joining: 4-09-2025
  • Last Date of Employment: 28-03-2026
  • Notice period served: 30 days
  • Last Drawn Salary: 20,000

The Department Supervisor, IT, Resources, Key Control, Library, and Operations all signed off. The Principal signed and dated the form 30/03/2026, with the note "No Balance Outstanding". I signed it back at 11:21 AM the same morning — noting only that the ID card would be returned when conditions allowed.

Why this is decisive: The school cannot, on the same morning, tell me in one document that my last day is 28 March with 30 days of notice served, and then tell me in another document (the EOS) that my last day is 26 March. One of these is wrong — and the clearance is signed by HR, the Principal, and multiple department heads.

7. The first EOS — sent three days after my last day, with arithmetic that doesn't add up

Roughly two hours after the clearance was signed — at 12:19 PM on 31 March 2026 — HR sent the first EOS. It stated:

  • Last Working Day: 26-03-2026 (two days before the clearance date)
  • March salary includes notice period — 26 days: AED 17,333
  • February salary includes notice period 3 days (26, 27, 28 Feb)

Even on the school's own framing, 26 + 3 = 29 days, not the 30 days the statute requires. The arithmetic fails before you even reach the legal argument.

8. I engaged respectfully, cited MOHRE, and gave the school every chance to fix it

The same afternoon I replied, attaching the resignation email and the signed clearance, and pointing out both the date error and the counting error. HR replied on 2 April that the settlement was "final." I then called MOHRE on 800 84 and obtained official clarification, which I relayed in writing on 2 April at 3:49 PM, and invited the school to call MOHRE directly to verify. I followed up again on 6 April.

MOHRE confirmed: a notice period legally commences the day after the resignation is submitted. Since I resigned on 26 February, my 30-day notice concludes exactly on 28 March.

9. The second EOS — still wrong, by one day

On 6 April at 1:58 PM, HR sent a second EOS. The last working day moved from 26 March to 27 March, and March salary moved from AED 17,333 to AED 18,000 (27 days). Sakha Al Dbaisi (Athena HR) later wrote that February "included three days (26, 27, and 28)" and that March therefore "represents remaining 27 days."

That still fails MOHRE's rule that notice begins the day after notification. Counting 26 February cannot be reconciled with the Labour Law — and it directly contradicts the verbal guidance MOHRE gave me on 800 84. I declined to sign and invoked Article 53, which requires the settlement within 14 days.

10. The school closed the door; I preserved my rights

On 7 April 2026 Sakha Al Dbaisi wrote: "Unfortunately, it's full and final as I mentioned in my email. You can proceed with MOHRE ticket and legal department will complete the process further." The school then transferred the file to their legal department.

I observed the 14-day grace period under Article 53, which concluded on 11 April 2026, and then opened this MOHRE complaint. I would still prefer to resolve this amicably today — the amount in dispute is modest. My objective is a correctly dated EOS that matches the clearance the school's own HR signed.

What I am asking the mediator to help achieve

  1. Last working day corrected to 28 March 2026 on the EOS, to match the signed clearance form.
  2. March salary corrected to AED 18,666.67 (28 days of the statutory 30-day notice period).
  3. Work permit cancellation processed in line with MOHRE's 14-day rule under Article 53.
  4. No waiver clauses in the EOS that would prevent me from approaching MOHRE — I have already filed, and my signature only certifies correct amounts.
