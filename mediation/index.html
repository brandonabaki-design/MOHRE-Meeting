The case in one sentence

I served my full 30-day statutory notice period under Article 9(3) of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, my last day was Saturday, 28 March 2026 (confirmed in writing on my signed clearance form), yet the school calculated my end-of-service settlement as if my last day were 27 March 2026 — short-paying me by 2 days and refusing to correct the document despite MOHRE's guidance.

Three undisputed facts that win this case

  1. Signed clearance form prepared by the school's own HR lists my Last Date of Employment as 28-03-2026 and the notice served as "30 days". The Principal also signed and dated it 30/03/2026 with "No Balance Outstanding".
  2. My resignation email of 26 Feb 2026 explicitly stated 28 March 2026 as my final day. The school never objected, never responded, and never proposed a different date at any point during the 30-day notice period.
  3. MOHRE's own rule is that a notice period begins the day after the notification. Counting from 27 Feb, the 30th day lands exactly on 28 March — which is why I picked that date in the first place.

Three soundbites to have ready

If asked what I want I am asking for what the law already requires: payment for the final two days of my statutory notice period — 28 days of March salary — and an EOS document that matches my signed clearance form.
If accused of playing games with dates I did not pick 28 March to gain anything. I picked it because MOHRE confirmed a notice period begins the day after the resignation is submitted. My resignation went in on 26 February — 30 calendar days later is 28 March.
If the school says their EOS is "full and final" The school's own signed clearance document, signed by HR and the Principal, says my last day was 28 March and that I served 30 days of notice. The EOS contradicts the school's own records.
