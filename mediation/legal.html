Legal Basis

The articles I have cited from day one, plus MOHRE's verbal guidance I obtained on 800 84.

Statutory framework

The relationship is governed by Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 concerning the Regulation of Labour Relations and its Executive Regulations. The contract itself (Article 4) explicitly states that any term conflicting with FDL 33/2021 is null and void. That clause was drafted by the employer.

Article 9, Clause 3 — Resignation during probation to join another UAE employer

Under Article 9(3) of FDL 33/2021, an employee wishing to leave during the probation period to move to another employer inside the UAE must notify their current employer in writing at least one month (30 days) in advance of the proposed end date.

"As I am currently within my probationary period and will be joining another employer within the UAE, I am submitting this resignation in accordance with Article 9, Clause 3 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021. Under this article, I am providing the mandatory 30-day notice period." — my resignation email, 26 Feb 2026

This article was cited in my resignation from the first sentence. The school cannot claim surprise about the legal basis or the notice length. I complied with it exactly.

Article 43 — Notice periods are calculated in calendar days

Article 43 of FDL 33/2021 requires that notice periods for termination run in calendar days. That means Fridays, Saturdays, public holidays, and school breaks all count toward the notice period. An employer cannot carve them out.

This matters because before I resigned, HR verbally told me I would not be paid for spring break. That verbal position is not compatible with Article 43, which is precisely why I took the care to cite it in writing in my resignation.

"As per Article 43 of the Labor Law, notice periods are calculated in calendar days, meaning my notice period will run continuously through the upcoming Spring Break closure." — my resignation email

Article 53 — Final settlement within 14 days

Article 53 (and its related Executive Regulations) requires the employer to pay all entitlements — final salary, gratuity, leave pay, etc. — within 14 days of the end of the contract, and to cancel the work permit within the same period.

My contract ended on 28 March 2026. The 14-day window therefore expired on 11 April 2026. I observed that grace period in full before filing this complaint, which is a matter of record in my 7 April email to Sakha Al Dbaisi.

MOHRE call-centre guidance (800 84)

I called 800 84 multiple times during this process. The consistent guidance I received was:

  • A notice period commences the day after the resignation notification is submitted, not the same day.
  • 30 days means 30 calendar days — breaks and rest days count.
  • If a settlement remains uncorrected for 14 days after the contract end date, the employee should open a MOHRE ticket.
  • Any clause in an EOS that waives the employee's right to approach MOHRE is not enforceable for amounts unpaid in violation of the Labour Law.

I relayed this guidance in writing to the school on 2 April 2026 and invited them to call MOHRE themselves. They did not take up the invitation; they instead declared the settlement "full and final."

Silence as acceptance — why the missing response to my resignation matters

My resignation email specified the end date (28 March 2026) in plain language, cited the governing articles, and went to the Principal with cc to HR and both Vice Principals. Under UAE labour practice, an employer receiving such a notice who continues to accept the employee's work, handover, and presence, and raises no written objection during the notice period, has no basis to unilaterally re-characterise the end date afterwards.

I am not asking the mediator to rule on silence-as-acceptance as a doctrine. I am pointing out that the school's own signed clearance form, issued at the end of the notice period, corroborates the 28 March date. Silence during the 30 days plus a signed clearance at the end of the 30 days is not a basis for a different date to appear in the EOS two hours later.

Waiver / "full and final" language in the EOS

The proposed EOS contains paragraphs stating that by accepting the payment I waive any right to approach the Ministry of Labour or the UAE courts. A waiver of statutory rights cannot cure an underpayment of statutory entitlements. The correct order is:

  1. Amount is made correct on the face of the document (28 March, AED 18,666.67).
  2. Payment is transferred to my bank account.
  3. Only then can any release or acknowledgement be signed.
I will not sign a "full and final" release against a figure that is known to be below the statutory entitlement. That is precisely what Article 53 protects against.

What the law requires the mediator to consider

  • Did the employee give a compliant 30-day notice? Yes — 26 Feb to 28 March inclusive of 30 calendar days.
  • Did the employer object during the notice? No written objection exists on record.
  • Did the employer issue a signed document confirming the end date? Yes — the clearance form, HR's handwriting, Principal's signature.
  • Has the employer paid for the full statutory notice? No — either 29 days (first EOS) or 29 days effectively (second EOS, miscounted).
  • Has the Article 53 deadline passed? Yes — 11 April 2026.
